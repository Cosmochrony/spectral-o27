  \begin{abstract}
    Let $\Phi_{q,\rho}$ be a morphism out of the Weil pair space $V_q$ of
    $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, subject to three constraints imposed by the
    admissible projection $\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$: involution equivariance, quadratic compatibility
    with $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}$, and factorisation through the admissible quotient.
    The third gives unique real-linear factorisation through
    $\mathcal{Q} = V_q / \ker \Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$ and nothing more; a countermodel shows that the
    three together impose no equivariance.

    Write $\mathrm{Ntrl}$ for the neutral traceless part of the ambient observable space.
    Reaching $H_{\mathrm{eff}} = \mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}} \cap \mathrm{Ntrl}$ from that
    quotient and transferring O23's dimension count to it are one hypothesis,
    $\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}} = \mathrm{Ntrl}$, assumed and derived nowhere.
    Under it and the spinor carrier supplied conditionally by paper O23, $H_{\mathrm{eff}} \simeq
    \mathfrak{su}(2)$ and the representation action gives a canonical equivariant map
    $\mathfrak{su}(2) \to \mathrm{End}(V_\rho)$ with one-dimensional complex intertwiner space, so
    an equivariant map is unique only up to a scalar that the quadratic constraint, a
    proportionality, does not fix.
    No non-zero $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-equivariant map reaches the fundamental representation from the
    adjoint, $\dim \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{su}(2)}(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{2}) = 0$, excluding an
    equivariant adjoint-factorising realisation of O26 Hypothesis 4.4 but not every
    quotient-factorising linear lift; that hypothesis therefore remains open, and the
    $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ identification remains supplied rather than derived.
    A second assumption makes the parity involution descend and act by $\mathrm{Ad}(g_0)$.

    Whether the canonical map satisfies the quadratic constraint is not settled: no result here
    makes the quotient map an isometry.
    Subject to it, $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$ would acquire a representation carrier in that sector;
    it does not acquire a derived interpretation of $\beta^*$, which has no native carrier on the
    Heisenberg substrate.

    \emph{Interpretation, not result}: the non-abelian carrier is an input to this programme's
    representation layer, not an output of the constraints, which supply an exact calculus on it.
  \end{abstract}

  \paragraph{Keywords.}
    Emergence; non-injectivity; admissible factorisation; Schur intertwiners; su(2);
    Weil representation; Born–Infeld; spectral admissibility

  \section{Introduction}

  \subsection{The problem: existence of an admissible morphism}

    Paper O26~\cite{Beau2026a30} identified the pair observable $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n) =
  \sigma_c(n) \cdot \sigma_{q-c}(n)$ as the Hilbert--Schmidt norm squared of a rank-one operator
    in $\mathrm{End}(V_\rho)$ for some representation $\rho$ of $2I \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)$.
    This identification rests on the existence of a morphism $\Phi_{q,\rho}$ out of $V_q$
    satisfying three structural constraints:
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item[(a)] \emph{Involution equivariance}: $\mathbb{Z}_2$-equivariance under $c \leftrightarrow
      q - c$, the involution acting on $V_q$ by complex conjugation.
      That it descends to $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ at all, and that the descended map is
      $\mathrm{Ad}(g_0)$ for some $g_0 \in \mathrm{SU}(2)$, are \emph{assumed}
      (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:parity}) and neither is derived here;
      \item[(b)] \emph{Quadratic compatibility}: $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n) \sim
      \|\Phi_{q,\rho}(v^{(n)}_c)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 \cdot
      \|\Phi_{q,\rho}(v^{(n)}_{q-c})\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$, the Hilbert--Schmidt norm being the one
      carried by the target $\mathrm{End}(V_\rho)$;
      \item[(c)] \emph{Factorisation through admissibility}: $\ker \Pi_{\mathrm{adm}} \subseteq \ker
      \Phi_{q,\rho}$.
      This is exactly the condition for unique linear factorisation through the admissibility
      quotient.
      It does \emph{not} imply frame independence, and it does not imply
      $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-equivariance;
    \end{enumerate}
    Constraint~(c) is a hypothesis on $\Phi_{q,\rho}$, not a consequence of the other two.
    Conditions~(a)--(c) are the reading used in this paper; they are not verbatim the clauses of
    O26 Hypothesis~4.4.
    That hypothesis asks for a canonical linear map
    \[
      \Phi_{q,\rho} \colon \mathcal{V}_q \longrightarrow V_\rho ,
    \]
    where $\mathcal{V}_q$ denotes, in the notation of O26, the space generated by the admissible
    Weil-block residual vectors $\{v^{(n)}_c\}$ equipped with the parity involution, and where the
    target is the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ representation space itself.
    Its naturality clause asks for independence of the pair $\{c, q-c\}$ and of the shell
    index~$n$; condition~(c) above replaces that clause by the kernel condition.
    The two are different requirements, and neither is derived from the other here.
    O26 Hypothesis~4.4 remains open.
    Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}(iv) excludes only an $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-equivariant
    adjoint-to-fundamental map; condition~(c) alone yields a unique linear map out of the quotient
    and puts no representation action on it.

  \subsection{The answer: a factorisation and a Schur count}

    The content of the paper is the following.
    Under constraint~(c), $\Phi_{q,\rho}$ factors through the admissible quotient
    $\mathcal{Q} = V_q / \ker \Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$.
    Identifying $\mathcal{Q}$ with the admissible neutral traceless sector $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is a
    further hypothesis (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation}), and under it together with the spinor
    carrier supplied by O23~\cite{Beau2026a27} that sector is $\mathfrak{su}(2)$; the
    representation action then supplies the canonical map $\rho \colon \mathfrak{su}(2) \to
    \mathrm{End}(V_\rho)$, whose intertwiner space is one-dimensional.
    The vector target $V_\rho$ is unreachable \emph{equivariantly}: the adjoint and the fundamental
    are inequivalent irreducibles.  Non-equivariant linear lifts are not excluded.

  \subsection{Relation to the representation-side O-series chain}

    O24~\cite{Beau2026a28} proves that vertical fibre structure does not perturb the
    observable rank.  The present result adds a representation-theoretic layer to the
    transfer-free implication chain
    \begin{align*}
      \text{emergence} &\Rightarrow \text{non-injectivity} \Rightarrow \text{pair structure}\\
      &\Rightarrow \text{quadratic form} \Rightarrow \mathfrak{su}(2),
    \end{align*}
    whose arrows are of unequal type (Remark~\ref{rem:chain}): the first is the no-go theorem of
    paper~L~\cite{Beau2026l}, the second rests on a structurally motivated hypothesis of
    O18~\cite{Beau2026a22}, the third is established in O26~\cite{Beau2026a30}, and the last is the
    carrier supplied conditionally by O23~\cite{Beau2026a27}.
    Subject to condition~(b), which this paper leaves open (Remark~\ref{rem:norm-open}), the
    exponent $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$ would describe a Hilbert--Schmidt trajectory in the minimal
    admissible non-abelian sector $\mathfrak{su}(2)$.
    No capacity-to-rate conclusion follows from this chain.

  \subsection{Scope}

    This paper proves the factorisation statement (Section~\ref{sec:rigidity}) and the canonical
    construction (Section~\ref{sec:construction}).
    It does not execute the numerical tests of O26 (those require O25 data~\cite{Beau2026a29}).
    It does not posit or repair the LPS-to-Heisenberg capacity-to-rate transfer.
    It does not derive $H_{\mathrm{eff}} \simeq \mathfrak{su}(2)$ from constraints~(a)--(c): that
    identification is the carrier supplied by O23~\cite{Beau2026a27}.
    It does not prove that the admissible quotient $\mathcal{Q}$ is the admissible neutral traceless
    sector $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$; that is Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation}, carried explicitly
    wherever it is used.
    It does not furnish the vector lift $\mathcal{V}_q \to V_\rho$ of O26 Hypothesis~4.4;
    Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}(iv) excludes an equivariant adjoint-factorising realisation of it,
    not every quotient-factorising linear lift.

  \subsection{Organisation}

    Section~\ref{sec:setup} fixes notation and recalls the relevant results from O18--O24.
    Section~\ref{sec:exclusion} states the universal property of the admissible quotient.
    Section~\ref{sec:rigidity} states and proves the factorisation and the Schur counts.
    Section~\ref{sec:construction} exhibits the canonical factorisation and proves uniqueness.
    Section~\ref{sec:chain} states the representation-side result and the transfer boundary.
    Section~\ref{sec:open} states the four remaining open problems.

  \section{Setup and Notation}
  \label{sec:setup}

  Let $q$ be an odd prime.
  For each non-trivial central character, indexed by $c \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$, the
  associated representation space of $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ is
  $V_c \simeq \mathbb{C}^q$, and conjugate characters give $V_{q-c} \simeq \overline{V_c}$.
  These are distinct representation spaces indexed by $c$, not summands of a single
  $\mathbb{C}^q$.
  Each $V_c$ also carries the Weil representation of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, which
  normalises the Heisenberg action on the same space; $V_c$ is the \emph{Weil block} at $c$ in the
  vocabulary of O19~\cite{Beau2026a23}, and the residual vectors $v^{(n)}_c$ of condition~(b) are
  its Weil-block residuals.
  The pair space is $V_q := V_c \oplus V_{q-c}$, of complex dimension $2q$; it is the Weil pair
  space named in the title.

  The parity involution $c \leftrightarrow q-c$, the candidate fibre structure of the pair
  sector (a structurally motivated hypothesis; O18~\cite{Beau2026a22} establishes
  Born--Infeld parity as a covariance of the response family and states the fibre identification as an
  open problem), acts on $V_q$ by complex conjugation:
  $V_{q-c} \simeq \overline{V_c}$ (the conjugation identity of O17, recalled in O18
  Theorem~3.1).
  The pair observable used here is
  \[
    \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n) := \sigma_c(n) \cdot \sigma_{q-c}(n),
    \qquad \sigma_c(n) = \delta r_n / |S_n|;
  \]
  it is the uncorrected pair observable, not the canonical normalisation proposed in
  O19~\cite{Beau2026a23}, whose independence tests are open.

  Two distinct objects appear below, and they are kept apart throughout.
  The admissible projection is written $\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$ everywhere, including in the abstract.
  It is a real-linear map from $V_q$ into the ambient observable space fixed below; under the spinor
  carrier that ambient space is $\mathrm{End}(V_\rho)$, so
  $\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}} \colon V_q \to \mathrm{End}(V_\rho)$.
  Its construction is not supplied here, and Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation} below is a statement
  about its image.

  The first object is the \emph{admissible quotient}
  \[
    \mathcal{Q} \;:=\; V_q / \ker \Pi_{\mathrm{adm}},
    \qquad \pi \colon V_q \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{Q} ,
  \]
  which is the only object the universal property of Section~\ref{sec:exclusion} produces.
  Every statement about maps out of $V_q$ in this paper is a statement about
  \emph{real}-linear maps: the parity involution acts by complex conjugation and is not
  complex linear, so $\mathbb{C}$-linearity is not available on the source.

  The second object is the \emph{admissible neutral traceless sector}
  \[
    H_{\mathrm{eff}} \;:=\; \mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}} \cap \mathrm{Ntrl},
  \]
  where $\mathrm{Ntrl}$ denotes the neutral traceless part of the ambient observable space in which
  $\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$ takes its values.
  Neither $\mathrm{Ntrl}$ nor that ambient space is constructed here; both arrive with the spinor
  carrier supplied by O23~\cite{Beau2026a27}, under which the ambient space is
  $\mathrm{End}(V_\rho)$ with $V_\rho \cong \mathbb{C}^2$, and
  \[
    \mathrm{Ntrl} = \mathfrak{su}(V_\rho) = \{ A \in \mathrm{End}(V_\rho) :
    A^\dagger = -A, \ \mathrm{Tr}\,A = 0 \} .
  \]
  What O23 Theorem~3.1~\cite{Beau2026a27} supplies is a dimension for $\mathrm{Ntrl}$:
  $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathfrak{su}(V_\rho) = 3$, and $\mathfrak{su}(V_\rho) \cong \mathfrak{su}(2)
  \cong \mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H}$ as a real Lie algebra, conditionally on the carrier selection,
  which remains an open bridge of O23.
  It supplies no statement about $\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$, about $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$, or
  about the admissible quotient: those objects do not occur there.
  Since $H_{\mathrm{eff}} \subseteq \mathrm{Ntrl}$ by definition, transferring that count to
  $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is equivalent to the inclusion
  $\mathrm{Ntrl} \subseteq \mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$, which is a separate assumption.

  \begin{hypothesis}[Admissible saturation]
    \label{hyp:saturation}
    The admissible projection saturates the neutral traceless sector:
    \[
      \mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}} \;=\; \mathrm{Ntrl}.
    \]
  \end{hypothesis}

  Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation} is not proved here and does not follow from the universal
  property.
  Lemma~\ref{lem:quotient} delivers $\mathcal{Q}$, canonically isomorphic to
  $\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$ and to nothing else; neither inclusion between
  $\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$ and $\mathrm{Ntrl}$ is established in this paper or in the
  sources it cites.
  It is the single identification on which everything beyond Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}(i) that
  mentions $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ depends, and it is recorded among the open problems in
  Section~\ref{sec:open}.
  Granting it, and granting the carrier:
  \[
    \mathcal{Q} \;\cong\; \mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}} \;=\; \mathrm{Ntrl}
    \;=\; \mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}} \cap \mathrm{Ntrl} \;=\; H_{\mathrm{eff}}
    \;=\; \mathfrak{su}(V_\rho),
  \]
  every step canonical, so $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} H_{\mathrm{eff}} = 3$ and $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ carries
  the Lie bracket of $\mathfrak{su}(V_\rho)$.
  Its complexification $H_{\mathrm{eff}} \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{C}$ is then a
  three-dimensional complex space, and no dimension count of this paper transfers from the one to
  the other in either direction.
  Where this paper calls $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ real three-dimensional, it is under
  Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation}.

  Under that hypothesis and the carrier, O23 supplies the quaternionic identification
  \[
    \iota \colon H_{\mathrm{eff}} \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;} \mathfrak{su}(2)
  \]
  \emph{as an isomorphism of real Lie algebras}, the bracket on $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ being the one it
  inherits from $\mathfrak{su}(V_\rho)$.
  Read that way, $\iota$ is unique up to $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-conjugacy, since
  $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{su}(2)) = \mathrm{Inn}(\mathfrak{su}(2)) \cong \mathrm{SO}(3)$.
  Read as a merely linear isomorphism it would be unique only up to $\mathrm{GL}(3,\mathbb{R})$ and
  the conjugacy statement would be false; the Lie-algebra reading is the one used throughout, and it
  is part of what the carrier supplies rather than something derived here.

  A second assumption concerns the parity involution.

  \begin{hypothesis}[Parity transport]
    \label{hyp:parity}
    Assume Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation}, so that $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is reached from $V_q$ at all.
    The parity involution of $V_q$ then descends to $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$, and the descended map is
    $P = \mathrm{Ad}(g_0)$ for a non-central $g_0 \in \mathrm{SU}(2)$.
  \end{hypothesis}

  Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:parity} presupposes Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation} in order to be
  formulated, since a descent to $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ requires a map onto it; it does not follow from
  it.

  It is not derived here.
  A general linear involution of a real three-dimensional space is not of that form: a reflection
  with a one-dimensional $(-1)$-eigenspace has determinant $-1$ and is therefore $\mathrm{Ad}(g)$
  for no $g$.
  The non-centrality clause excludes $g_0 = \pm\mathrm{Id}$, for which $P$ would be the identity;
  the results below survive that degenerate case a fortiori, since every orthogonal map commutes
  with the identity, but the arguments are written for the non-degenerate one.
  What the setting does settle is what the descended involution cannot be.
  It is not $X \mapsto -X$: on a non-abelian Lie algebra the negation map is an anti-automorphism,
  since $[-X,-Y] = [X,Y]$.
  And the lift is of order four, not two: in $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ the only element of order two is
  $-\mathrm{Id}$, whose adjoint action is trivial, whereas a non-trivial $\pi$-rotation in
  $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ lifts to $g_0$ with $g_0^2 = -\mathrm{Id}$, for instance $g_0 = i\sigma_2$.

  The admissibility thread $Q_8 \subset 2I \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)$ selects a spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$
  representation $\rho$ as the minimal non-abelian candidate (O26~\cite{Beau2026a30},
  Section~3.3).

  \begin{definition}[Factorisation through admissibility]
    \label{def:naturality}
    A morphism $\Phi \colon V_q \to W$ \emph{factors through admissibility} if
    \[
      \ker \Pi_{\mathrm{adm}} \subseteq \ker \Phi .
    \]
    Equivalently, $\Phi$ is constant on the fibres of the admissible quotient and descends uniquely
    to it.
    This is a statement about the kernel and nothing more: it does not make $\Phi$ independent of an
    auxiliary basis, BFS block or Gram--Schmidt frame, and it does not make $\Phi$ equivariant.
  \end{definition}

  \begin{remark}[Why the condition is stated on the kernel]
    \label{rem:kernel-form}
    Requiring instead invariance under every linear $f \colon V_q \to V_q$ with $f(\ker
    \Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}) \subseteq \ker \Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$ would include $f = 0$ and force
    $\Phi = 0$.
    Definition~\ref{def:naturality} is the quotient condition, and it is the condition used
    throughout this paper.
  \end{remark}

  Throughout, $V_\rho$ denotes a complex representation space of dimension $d_\rho$, endowed with
  its natural Hilbert structure, carrying a representation $\rho$ of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ whose
  restriction to $2I \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)$ is the representation selected by the admissibility
  thread.
  Both readings are used below: the Schur counts require the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ one, and the
  admissibility thread supplies a $\rho$ that is both.

  \begin{remark}[Non-arbitrariness of $\rho$ via the admissibility thread]
    \label{rem:thread}
    The representation $\rho$ is not chosen freely.
    The admissibility thread
    \[
      Q_8 \;\subset\; 2I \;\subset\; \mathrm{SU}(2)
    \]
    is imposed structurally: $Q_8$ is the minimal non-abelian finite subgroup of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$
    compatible with bounded-flux admissibility~\cite{Beau2026a1}; $2I$ is the maximal admissible
    finite subgroup, distinguished by the isotropy of its second-moment tensor among all binary
    polyhedral groups~\cite{Beau2026a2}; and the LPS family provides the Ramanujan approximation
    to $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ in the large-$q$ limit~\cite{Beau2026a1}.
    The spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ sector is selected because $Q_8 \subset 2I$ acts on $V_{\rho_{1/2}}
    \simeq \mathbb{C}^2$ as the minimal non-abelian representation, yielding the smallest
    non-trivial Laplacian eigenvalue $\lambda_{\rho_4} = 6 < 8 = \lambda_{\mathrm{ab}}$ and hence
    the widest admissibility window~\cite{Beau2026a1,Beau2026a30}.
    The choice of $\rho$ is therefore an output of the admissibility hierarchy, not an input.
  \end{remark}

  \section{The Admissible Quotient}
  \label{sec:exclusion}

  Condition~(c) has exactly one consequence, and it is the universal property of the quotient.

  \begin{lemma}[Universality of the admissible quotient]
    \label{lem:quotient}
    Let $\pi \colon V_q \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{Q} = V_q / \ker \Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$ be the
    admissible quotient map.
    Any morphism $\Phi \colon V_q \to W$ satisfying condition~(c) factors uniquely through $\pi$:
    there exists a unique real-linear $\bar{\Phi} \colon \mathcal{Q} \to W$ such that
    $\Phi = \bar{\Phi} \circ \pi$.
    The target of $\pi$ is $\mathcal{Q}$ and not $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$; the two are identified only
    under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation}.
  \end{lemma}

  \begin{proof}
    Condition~(c) is $\ker \Pi_{\mathrm{adm}} \subseteq \ker \Phi$, so $\Phi$ is constant on the
    fibres of $\pi$ and descends uniquely to $\mathcal{Q}$.
    This is the universal property of $V_q / \ker \Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$: the lemma restates
    condition~(c) in the form used below and adds nothing to it.
    In particular it produces $\mathcal{Q}$, which is canonically isomorphic to
    $\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$, and no further intersection.
    The kernel $\ker \Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$ consists of the directions invisible to admissible
    observables, which is the structural reading of the condition.
  \end{proof}

  \section{Factorisation and the Admissible Action}
  \label{sec:rigidity}

  \begin{theorem}[Factorisation, and the equivariant Schur counts]
    \label{thm:rigidity}
    Let $\Phi_{q,\rho}$ be a morphism out of $V_q$ satisfying condition~(c).  Then:
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item[(i)] $\Phi_{q,\rho}$ factors uniquely, as a real-linear map, through the admissible
      quotient $\pi \colon V_q \to \mathcal{Q}$.
      This is condition~(c) restated; it carries no equivariance, and it produces $\mathcal{Q}$
      rather than $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
    \end{enumerate}
    Conditions~(a) and~(b) are not used anywhere in this theorem, and are therefore not assumed.
    Assume in addition the spinor carrier supplied by O23~\cite{Beau2026a27}, so that
    $V_\rho \cong \mathbb{C}^2$ and $\mathrm{Ntrl} = \mathfrak{su}(V_\rho)$.  Then:
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item[(ii)] under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation}, $H_{\mathrm{eff}} =
      \mathfrak{su}(V_\rho)$ and the representation action is a canonical equivariant map
      $\rho \colon \mathfrak{su}(2) \to \mathrm{End}(V_\rho)$;
      \item[(iii)] the complex intertwiner space, after complexifying the adjoint source,
      \[
        \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{su}(2)}(\mathbf{3}_{\mathbb{C}},\, \mathrm{End}(V_\rho))
      \]
      is one-dimensional, since
      $\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^2) \cong \mathbf{3} \oplus \mathbf{1}$ as an
      $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-module.
      Over $\mathbb{R}$, the space of real-linear intertwiners from real $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ into
      $\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^2)$ has real dimension two;
      \item[(iv)] there is no non-zero $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-equivariant map from the adjoint
      representation to the fundamental representation:
      \[
        \dim \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{su}(2)}(\mathbf{3}_{\mathrm{ad}},
        \mathbf{2}_{\mathrm{fund}}) = 0 ,
      \]
      the two being inequivalent irreducibles.
    \end{enumerate}
    Parts~(iii) and~(iv) use only the carrier: they are representation theory of
    $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ and $\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^2)$ and do not depend on
    Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation} or on Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:parity}.
    Statements~(iii) and~(iv) are statements about \emph{equivariant} maps.  They do not classify
    the maps that merely satisfy~(a)--(c); see Remark~\ref{rem:not-equivariant}.
  \end{theorem}

  \begin{proof}
    \textbf{(i).}
    Condition~(c) is $\ker \Pi_{\mathrm{adm}} \subseteq \ker \Phi_{q,\rho}$, which is the universal
    property of the quotient (Lemma~\ref{lem:quotient}), whose target is $\mathcal{Q}$.

    \textbf{(ii).}
    Under the carrier and Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation}, $H_{\mathrm{eff}} =
    \mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}} \cap \mathrm{Ntrl} = \mathrm{Ntrl} =
    \mathfrak{su}(V_\rho)$, and
    $\rho(\mathrm{Ad}(g)X) = \rho(g)\rho(X)\rho(g)^{-1}$, so $\rho$
    intertwines the adjoint action on the source with conjugation on the target.

    \textbf{(iii)} and \textbf{(iv)}.
    Both are Schur's lemma in the category of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-modules.  The source is the adjoint
    $\mathbf{3}$.  For $V_\rho \cong \mathbb{C}^2$ the fundamental $\mathbf{2}$ is irreducible and
    inequivalent to $\mathbf{3}$, giving~(iv).  Decomposing
    $\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^2) \cong \mathbf{2} \otimes \mathbf{2}^{*} \cong \mathbf{3} \oplus
    \mathbf{1}$ gives a one-dimensional complex space of intertwiners
    $\mathbf{3} \to \mathbf{3} \oplus \mathbf{1}$, which is the first half of~(iii).
    The real statement is not obtained by forgetting a complex structure on that Hom space, which
    would describe a different object; it follows from the adjunction
    $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(W, U) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(W \otimes_{\mathbb{R}}
    \mathbb{C}, U)$, valid for a real module $W$ and a complex one $U$, applied with
    $W = \mathfrak{su}(2)$ and $U = \mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^2)$: the right-hand side is the
    one-dimensional complex space just computed, of real dimension two.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}[Scope of the intertwiner counts]
    \label{rem:schur-scope}
    Part~(iii) is stated for the carrier's $V_\rho \cong \mathbb{C}^2$.
    The same counts hold for every non-trivial irreducible $\rho$ of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, that is for
    every $d_\rho \geq 2$: the adjoint $\mathbf{3}$ occurs in
    $\mathrm{End}(V_\rho) \cong V_\rho \otimes V_\rho^{*}$ with multiplicity one, by the
    Clebsch--Gordan decomposition, so the complex intertwiner space is one-dimensional and the
    real-linear one has real dimension two.
    They fail for the trivial representation $d_\rho = 1$, where $\rho|_{\mathfrak{su}(2)} = 0$
    and both spaces vanish.
    Every statement below that uses $\rho$ as a faithful map on $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ therefore
    assumes $d_\rho \geq 2$, which the admissibility thread supplies.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[Conditions~(a)--(c) do not impose equivariance]
    \label{rem:not-equivariant}
    Conditions~(a)--(c) impose one parity equivariance, a quadratic norm relation, and quotient
    factorisation.  None imposes equivariance under the full adjoint $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ action, and
    the Schur counts of Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}(iii)--(iv) therefore do not classify all
    admissible maps.

    A countermodel.  Assume Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:parity}, so the parity involution on the real
    adjoint carrier is $P = \mathrm{Ad}(g_0)$.
    Equip $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ with the quadratic form $X \mapsto \|\rho(X)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$,
    which for $d_\rho \geq 2$ is positive definite and $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariant, hence
    proportional to the standard Euclidean form by irreducibility of the adjoint, and therefore
    invariant under the full orthogonal group, reflections included.
    Being $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariant, the form makes $P$ orthogonal, so the eigenspaces of $P$ are
    mutually orthogonal; the $(-1)$-eigenspace of a non-trivial $\mathrm{Ad}(g_0)$ is
    two-dimensional.
    Let $T$ be a reflection inside that eigenspace, extended by the identity on the
    $(+1)$-eigenline: it is orthogonal, non-scalar, and commutes with $P$.
    Then
    \[
      \Phi_T := \rho \circ T \circ \iota \circ \pi
    \]
    has the same kernel, since $T$, $\iota$ and $\rho|_{\mathfrak{su}(2)}$ are injective, hence
    satisfies~(c); it has the same Hilbert--Schmidt norm, since $T$ is orthogonal for that form,
    hence satisfies~(b) whenever $\rho \circ \iota \circ \pi$ does; and it is parity
    equivariant, since $TP = PT$.
    It is not a scalar multiple of $\rho \circ \iota \circ \pi$, real or complex.
    A complex $\lambda$ is excluded first: $\rho(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ consists of anti-Hermitian
    operators, so $\rho(\mathfrak{su}(2)) \cap i\,\rho(\mathfrak{su}(2)) = 0$ and
    $\lambda$ must be real.
    A real $\lambda$ is then excluded because $T$ is not scalar and $\rho \circ \iota$ is
    injective for $d_\rho \geq 2$.

    Consequently the uniqueness statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} requires
    $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-equivariance of the descended map as an explicit hypothesis, and the
    impossibility statement~(iv) excludes an equivariant adjoint-factorising realisation of O26
    Hypothesis~4.4 --- it does not exclude every quotient-factorising linear lift.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[Relation to O23]
    \label{rem:o23}
    O23~\cite{Beau2026a27} proves $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathfrak{su}(V_\rho) = 3$ conditionally on
    the supplied spinor carrier (O23 Theorem~3.1), for $\mathrm{Ntrl}$ and not for
    $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
    Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} adds to this the factorisation~(i), which is condition~(c) and which
    concerns $\mathcal{Q}$, and the Schur counts~(iii)--(iv), which are representation theory of the
    supplied carrier.
    O23 proves $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathfrak{su}(V_\rho) = 3$; the transfer of that count to
    $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation}, and this paper adds no independent
    derivation of it, nor of $H_{\mathrm{eff}} \simeq \mathfrak{su}(2)$.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[What the three constraints do not determine]
    \label{rem:not-determined}
    Constraints~(a)--(c) do not by themselves single out $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ among three-dimensional
    real Lie algebras.
    A three-dimensional real \emph{non-abelian} Lie algebra carrying a positive-definite
    $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariant form is the compact form $\mathfrak{su}(2)$: $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariance
    makes every $\mathrm{ad}_X$ skew-symmetric for that form, so the algebra is of compact type and
    splits as $\mathfrak{z} \oplus [\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]$ with compact semisimple derived
    algebra; in dimension three the derived algebra has dimension $0$ or $3$, leaving the abelian
    $\mathbb{R}^3$ and $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ as the only possibilities.
    The non-abelian hypothesis is not decorative: $\mathbb{R}^3$ carries a positive-definite
    $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariant form as well, its adjoint action being trivial.
    Without $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariance the classification also admits $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$
    and the solvable types.
    Condition~(b) supplies a positive-definite quadratic form; whether that form is
    $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariant is not established here, and neither is non-abelianness of the carrier.
    Establishing both would identify the carrier without the supplied carrier of O23, and the pair
    is recorded as an open problem in Section~\ref{sec:open}.
  \end{remark}

  \section{Canonical Construction and Uniqueness}
  \label{sec:construction}

  \begin{definition}[Canonical admissible morphism]
    \label{def:Phi}
    Define
    \[
      \Phi_{q,\rho} \;:=\; \rho \circ \iota \circ \pi
      \;\colon\; V_q \longrightarrow \mathrm{End}(V_\rho),
    \]
    where $\pi \colon V_q \to \mathcal{Q}$ is the admissible quotient map, read as landing in
    $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation}, $\iota \colon
    H_{\mathrm{eff}} \xrightarrow{\,\simeq\,} \mathfrak{su}(2)$ is the quaternionic Lie-algebra
    identification of O23, and $\rho \colon \mathfrak{su}(2) \to \mathrm{End}(V_\rho)$ is the Lie
    algebra action of the chosen irreducible $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-representation.
    Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation} the isomorphism
    $\mathcal{Q} \cong H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is canonical, so $\Phi_{q,\rho}$ is well defined
    without a further choice.
    The definition therefore presupposes the carrier and Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation}.
    The target is $\mathrm{End}(V_\rho)$, the space in which $\rho$ takes its values.
  \end{definition}

  \begin{theorem}[The canonical action, and uniqueness among equivariant maps]
    \label{thm:main}
    Assume the carrier of O23 and Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation}.
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item[(1)] The morphism $\Phi_{q,\rho}$ of Definition~\ref{def:Phi} satisfies~(c)
      unconditionally, and satisfies~(a) under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:parity}.
      \item[(2)] Let $\Phi$ satisfy~(c), and let its descended map
      $\bar\Phi \colon H_{\mathrm{eff}} \to \mathrm{End}(V_\rho)$ be
      $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-equivariant for the adjoint action transported through $\iota$.
      The equivariance hypothesis is stated on $\bar\Phi$ because no
      $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-action on $V_q$ is defined anywhere in this paper.
      Then $\Phi = \lambda\,\Phi_{q,\rho}$ for a complex scalar $\lambda$.
      Condition~(b), being a proportionality, does not fix $\lambda$.
      \item[(3)] Without the equivariance hypothesis, (2) is false
      (Remark~\ref{rem:not-equivariant}).
    \end{enumerate}
    Whether $\Phi_{q,\rho}$ satisfies~(b) is not settled here; see Remark~\ref{rem:norm-open}.
  \end{theorem}

  \begin{proof}
    \textbf{(1).}
    (c) Each factor $\pi$, $\iota$, $\rho$ is defined on the quotient by construction, so
    $\ker \Phi_{q,\rho} \supseteq \ker \pi = \ker \Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$.
    (a) Granting Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:parity}, the involution acts on $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ by
    $\mathrm{Ad}(g_0)$, and $\rho(\mathrm{Ad}(g_0) X) = \rho(g_0)\,\rho(X)\,\rho(g_0)^{-1}$, so
    the $\mathbb{Z}_2$-action is intertwined at each stage.
    Without that assumption the descended involution is not available and~(a) is not established
    for $\Phi_{q,\rho}$; this is the same kind of gap that Remark~\ref{rem:norm-open} records
    for~(b).

    \textbf{(2).}
    By Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}(i) such a map factors as $\bar\Phi \circ \pi$, and under
    Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation} the descended map is defined on $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
    The hypothesis on $\bar\Phi$ makes it an intertwiner.
    Now $\rho \circ \iota$ and $i\,\rho \circ \iota$ are intertwiners, and they are
    $\mathbb{R}$-independent, their images being respectively anti-Hermitian and Hermitian, so they
    span a two-dimensional real subspace of the space of real-linear intertwiners, which by~(iii)
    has real dimension two.
    That space is therefore exactly
    $\{\lambda\,\rho \circ \iota : \lambda \in \mathbb{C}\}$, and
    $\bar\Phi = \lambda\,\rho \circ \iota$.
    The ambiguity in $\iota$ is $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-conjugacy, since $\iota$ is an isomorphism of
    real Lie algebras and $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{su}(2))$ is inner; that ambiguity is
    unobservable in the Hilbert--Schmidt norm.

    \textbf{(3).}
    Remark~\ref{rem:not-equivariant} exhibits $\Phi_T$ satisfying~(a) and~(c) and not of that form.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}[The quadratic condition is not established for the canonical map]
    \label{rem:norm-open}
    Condition~(b) requires the Hilbert--Schmidt norm along the trajectory to reproduce
    $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}$ up to a fixed scale.
    Neither kernel factorisation nor the universal property of the quotient makes $\pi$ an isometry
    onto $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$, and no lemma of this paper supplies one.
    Whether $\Phi_{q,\rho}$ satisfies~(b) therefore depends on a metric property of the admissible
    quotient that is stated nowhere, and it is recorded here as open.
    Corollary~\ref{cor:beta} is stated conditionally on it.
  \end{remark}

  \section{Representation Result and Transfer Boundary}
  \label{sec:chain}

  Condition~(b) asserts that the pair observable satisfies
  \[
    \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n) \;\sim\; \|\Phi_{q,\rho}(v^{(n)}_c) \otimes
    \Phi_{q,\rho}(v^{(n)}_{q-c})\|^2_{\mathrm{HS}} .
  \]
  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} does \emph{not} establish this proportionality for the canonical action:
  it is exactly condition~(b), left open by Remark~\ref{rem:norm-open}.
  If it holds, $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$ is the decay exponent of a Hilbert--Schmidt norm along a
  trajectory in $\mathrm{End}(V_\rho)$.

  \begin{corollary}[Representation carrier of $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$]
    \label{cor:beta}
    Assume the carrier of O23, Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation}, and that $\Phi_{q,\rho}$
    satisfies condition~(b) (Remark~\ref{rem:norm-open}).
    Then the exponent $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$ is the decay exponent of a Hilbert--Schmidt
    trajectory in a representation space factoring through the minimal admissible
    non-abelian sector $\mathfrak{su}(2)$.
    This statement does not imply $\beta^*=1/(\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}+\tfrac12)$.
    That reciprocal requires a distinct cross-substrate growth hypothesis, refuted for the
    two native realisations in the frozen corpus~\cite{Beau2026sgn}.
  \end{corollary}

  \begin{remark}[Realisation of $\pi$ is a separate matter]
    \label{rem:pi-realisation}
    Definition~\ref{def:Phi} uses the admissible quotient map $\pi$ as an abstract object.
    Approximating $\pi$ by the leading Gram--Schmidt basis vectors of the fitting window is not a
    realisation of it: O25~\cite{Beau2026a29} reports that this approximation yields
    $r_{\mathrm{eff}} = 2$ universally at $q \in \{29, 61\}$, an artefact of the DC mode dominating
    the projection, and records the correct implementation as an open problem.
    A depth-indexed, frame-independent realisation of $\pi$ is not supplied here.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[Type of the implication chain]
    \label{rem:chain}
    The chain
    \[
      \text{emergence} \Rightarrow \text{non-injectivity} \Rightarrow \text{pair structure}
      \Rightarrow \text{quadratic form} \Rightarrow \mathfrak{su}(2)
    \]
    has arrows of unequal type.
    The first is established in paper~L~\cite{Beau2026l}.
    The second is partly open: O18~\cite{Beau2026a22} proves Born--Infeld parity as a
    response-family covariance, but the identification of the parity orbit with a fibre of the
    non-injective projection of O18 is a structurally motivated hypothesis (O18 Problem~2.8).
    The third is established in O26~\cite{Beau2026a30}.
    The last is the supplied carrier of O23, read here through Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}(ii) and
    through Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation}; it is not derived from~(a)--(c).
  \end{remark}

  \section{Remaining Open Problems}
  \label{sec:open}

  Four problems are left open by this paper.

  \paragraph{The vector lift.}
  O26 Hypothesis~4.4~\cite{Beau2026a30} asks for a canonical linear map $\mathcal{V}_q \to V_\rho$
  into the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ representation space.
  Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}(iv) excludes an \emph{equivariant} adjoint-factorising realisation,
  since $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{su}(2)}(\mathbf{3}_{\mathrm{ad}}, \mathbf{2}_{\mathrm{fund}}) = 0$.
  It does not exclude a quotient-factorising linear lift that is not equivariant, nor a map that
  does not factor through the quotient at all.
  Classifying the admissible non-equivariant lifts, or reaching the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ sector by an
  observable of a different type, is open.

  \paragraph{The saturation hypothesis.}
  Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:saturation} asserts $\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}} = \mathrm{Ntrl}$.
  Neither inclusion is established in this paper or in the sources it cites, and the two halves fail
  differently.
  Without $\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}} \subseteq \mathrm{Ntrl}$ the admissible quotient
  $\mathcal{Q}$ is not the sector $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$, and the universal property of
  condition~(c) reaches no neutral traceless object at all.
  Without $\mathrm{Ntrl} \subseteq \mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$ the dimension count that O23
  proves for $\mathfrak{su}(V_\rho)$ does not transfer to $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$, which is then merely
  some subspace of a three-dimensional space.
  Every statement of this paper that mentions $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ passes through this hypothesis,
  its own presupposition in Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:parity} and in condition~(a) included;
  Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}(i), (iii) and~(iv) do not, being respectively the universal property
  and two Schur counts on the carrier.
  Supplying the hypothesis, or replacing it by a construction of $\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$ whose image is
  $\mathrm{Ntrl}$ by definition, is the cheapest available strengthening of the whole chain.

  \paragraph{Parity transport.}
  Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:parity} asserts that the parity involution descends to $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and
  acts there as $\mathrm{Ad}(g_0)$.
  Only the second half is constrained by anything proved here: the descended map cannot be
  $X \mapsto -X$, and its lift has order four.
  That the involution descends at all requires the parity map to preserve
  $\ker \Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$, which is nowhere established, and that the descent is inner requires it
  to be a Lie-algebra automorphism of determinant $+1$.
  Only condition~(a) of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(1) and the countermodel of
  Remark~\ref{rem:not-equivariant} depend on it.

  \paragraph{$\mathrm{Ad}$-invariance of the quadratic form.}
  Remark~\ref{rem:not-determined} isolates what would upgrade the carrier identification into a
  derivation: proving that the $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}$ form of condition~(b) is
  $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariant, and supplying a non-abelianness input for the carrier.
  The classification of three-dimensional real non-abelian Lie algebras with a positive-definite
  $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariant form would then yield $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ without the carrier supplied by
  O23, and would discharge part of the open bridge there.
  Neither ingredient is supplied by this paper.

  \subsection*{Interpretive outlook}

  What follows is a structural reading of the results above, offered as interpretation and not as a
  result of this paper.

  The paper narrows what the admissibility constraints can be asked to do.
  The three-dimensional non-abelian carrier is an input to the representation layer of the
  programme, supplied by O23 under an open bridge of its own, and not an output of the
  pair-observable constraints.
  What the constraints then supply, once that carrier is granted, is an exact internal calculus on
  it: the equivariant maps are counted, and the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ target is shown to be
  equivariantly unreachable from the adjoint.

  The distinction matters for how the representation-side chain should be read.
  A reader looking here for the origin of the non-abelian sector will not find it; a reader looking
  for what can be computed once that sector is granted will find it stated exactly, together with
  the ingredients whose absence keeps the identification conditional, the bridge from the
  admissibility quotient to that sector among them.
  On the same reading, the remaining vector lift is a question about which observable reaches the
  spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ sector at all, rather than a question about how to repair an equivariant map
  that the Schur count has shown cannot exist.
